# Evaluation Metrics for RAG Systems

Evaluating RAG is multi-dimensional. A system can retrieve well but generate poorly, or vice versa.

## Retrieval Metrics
Assuming a set of queries with known relevant document IDs (or passages):

- **Precision@k**: fraction of the top-k retrieved items that are relevant
- **Recall@k**: fraction of all relevant items that appear in the top-k
- **Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)**: average of 1/rank of the first relevant item
- **nDCG@k**: normalized discounted cumulative gain, sensitive to ranking quality

These require labeled relevance judgments. Synthetic labels can be generated by an LLM judge when human labels are scarce, but they introduce bias.

## Generation / End-to-End Metrics (RAGAS style)
- **Faithfulness**: Is every claim in the answer supported by the retrieved context?
- **Answer Relevancy**: Does the answer address the question?
- **Context Precision**: Are the retrieved contexts ranked such that relevant ones appear higher?
- **Context Recall**: Do the retrieved contexts contain the information needed to answer the question?

RAGAS uses an LLM-as-judge approach with carefully designed prompts. Results should always be validated on a human-labeled subset.

## Practical Advice
1. Build a small, high-quality golden set (50–200 examples) before optimizing.
2. Track both retrieval and generation metrics; optimizing only one often hurts the other.
3. Report confidence intervals or at least standard deviation across the eval set.
4. Document failure cases explicitly; they are more valuable than leaderboard numbers for real systems.
